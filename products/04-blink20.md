# Blink20

**Focus. Rest. Protect.**

Long days in front of a screen leave their mark on the eyes. Blink20 is a gentle, private companion built on the 20-20-20 rule that optometrists recommend: **every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.** It keeps time so you don't have to, and turns a healthy habit into an effortless one.

Blink20 began inside Uritect, as a tool for our own team. It helped us so much that keeping it to ourselves felt wrong, so we made it free for everyone.

## What you can do

### Start a focus session
Press **Start** and get back to work. Blink20 counts down to your next break and lets you know how long is left.

### Take a real break
When it is time, a calm rest screen asks you to look 20 feet away and counts down the seconds. A relaxing soundscape can play while you rest. Need to keep going? Hold to skip.

### Make it yours
- **Reminder interval:** how often you are reminded to rest.
- **Resting duration:** how long each break lasts.
- **Relaxing sound:** nature, fire, sea or waterfall, your own audio file, or silence.
- **Quiet hours:** no reminders during the hours you choose.
- **Dark mode:** a low-light palette that is easy on tired eyes.
- **Language:** 21 languages, including English, French, Spanish, Arabic, Persian, Hindi, Chinese and Japanese, or follow your device.

### See your progress
The **Stats** screen shows how much you rested today, this week, this month and this year. Charts break it down by hour, day, date or month; tap any bar for the details. Your session count, averages and your **streak** of consecutive days keep you motivated.

## Private by design

Everything Blink20 does happens on your device. There is no sign-up, no tracking and no data collection, and it works without an internet connection.

## Getting started

1. Download Blink20 for your phone or computer.
2. Follow the three-card introduction and allow notifications, so reminders can reach you.
3. Press **Start**. Blink20 takes care of the rest.

## Good to know

Blink20 supports healthier screen habits. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat any condition. If your eyes trouble you, please see an optometrist.
